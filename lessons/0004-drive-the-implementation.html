The trap: "now build it all"

You finish a clean grill. The plan is tight, the terms are decided, the trade-off is recorded. So you type "great, implement all of it" and let it run unattended. That is an autonomous agent workflow by another name — the exact thing this mission rules out — and the cost is concrete: a large diff you never watched take shape, stuffed with micro-decisions you didn't see made. Now you must reverse-engineer those decisions in review. Or worse, your colleague does, in comments.

Grilling moved the context cost upstream so it'd be cheap. Handing over the wheel at implementation drags it right back downstream. The plan was supposed to be spent in the open, not cashed in behind your back.

The shift: spend the plan in small, watched increments

The grill already drew the design tree. Implementation walks that tree one branch at a time, and you read every diff before authorising the next. The model's hands are on the keyboard; yours stay on the wheel. You are the author of intent — it is merely the author of syntax. That division is the whole discipline of "human-in-the-loop."

Five moves that keep you driving

Slice the plan into reviewable steps

You don't need to invent the slices — the grill produced them. Each resolved branch of the design tree is a step. Implement one, not the tree in a single generation. Small steps keep each diff inside the span you can actually hold in your head.

If a step is too big to read, it was too big to generate

Cap each generation at what you will genuinely review line by line. A diff you skim is a diff you didn't review — and it carries exactly the unseen micro-decisions you ran the grill to eliminate. When a generation overflows that budget, that's the signal to slice finer, not to trust harder.

Re-read every step's diff and adjust on the spot

Each generated step gets a real read, not a skim — eyeball the diff as a reviewer, not its author, while the context is still hot and the change is only one step wide. When something's off — a name, a shortcut, an assumption you didn't authorise — ask the question or make the correction right there, before you authorise the next step. An adjustment made now is a keystroke; the same fix after five more steps land on top is an unpick. The next two moves are the corrections you'll make most.

Hold the model to the decided language and decisions

When a generation drifts from a CONTEXT.md term — calls it Route when you decided Trip — or quietly contradicts an ADR's choice, that is a stop-and-correct on the spot, not a review comment for later. The artifacts from Lesson 3 are only worth the ink if you enforce them while the code is being written.

A new question mid-implementation means stop and grill

If a step surfaces a decision the grill never resolved, you've found a missing branch — not an excuse for the model to pick a plausible default and move on. Stop. Grill that branch the same way you grilled the rest, then resume. A guess papered over here is a wrong assumption your reviewer inherits.

An autonomous run optimises for finishing. You optimise for a diff your reviewer approves on the first pass. Those are not the same objective — and only one of them is yours to defend.

Staying the driver isn't slower

It feels slower because the effort is visible. But every step you watch is a review comment that never gets written, a wrong term never calcified, a guessed default never shipped. The grill front-loaded the thinking; controlled implementation front-loads the catching. Both move cost off your colleague's desk and onto your own keyboard, where it's cheapest to pay.

Your win for today

Implement one grilled change without a single "build it all" prompt

Take a change you've already grilled (Lessons 1–3) and implement it under your own hand:

  1. Slice it into steps you can each read in full. Drive one step, read the diff, then authorise the next — never the whole tree at once.
  2. Catch one moment the model drifts from a CONTEXT.md term or an ADR's choice. Correct it on the spot, before the next step — not in a comment later.
  3. Catch one moment a step surfaces an unresolved decision. Stop, grill that branch, then resume. Don't let it guess past you.
