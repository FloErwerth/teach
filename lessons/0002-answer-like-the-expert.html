Answer like the expert in the room

A grilling session is only as good as your answers. The skill supplies the questions and a recommendation — your edge is knowing which questions only you can answer, and answering those with precision.

Mission: front-load the context only you hold

The trap: answering everything yourself

Now that you can invoke a grill, the new failure mode is treating it like an oral exam where you must answer every question. You don't. Some questions the model should verify itself; some it has already answered correctly; and a precious few only you can answer. Spending equal effort on all three is how a good session turns slow and shallow.

Triage every question into one bucket

① Verifiable — the answer's in the repo. Don't recite it; tell it to read the code. ② Proposed-and-right — its recommendation matches yours; approve and move on. ③ Intent / constraint — product intent, business rules, external contracts, unwritten team conventions. The model cannot read these. This is the bucket your session exists for.

The whole game is routing fast through ① and ②, so your attention pools on ③.

The full version lives on your new Grilling Session Playbook reference card — print it and keep it beside the terminal.

A fourth outcome: some questions have no verbal answer

Sometimes a question fits none of the three buckets because it can't be settled in words at all. "Should this form be one screen or three?" is high-fidelity — you need to see it before you can answer. Forcing a reply in chat just bakes in a guess. The move is a handoff loop: grill → prototype → grill again. Park that branch, build the smallest throwaway prototype that answers it (well worth it for RN/Expo UI), then carry the answer back into the session and keep resolving the low-fidelity branches as normal. Trying to resolve high-fidelity questions by conversation alone is one of the classic grilling mistakes.

Two moves that multiply the signal

Reject with reasons

When the recommended answer is wrong, the reason is the highest-value thing you can say. "No" makes it guess again. "No, because the trip cache must survive a cold app launch, so it can't live in memory" gives it a constraint it will respect for the rest of the session — and one your reviewer would otherwise have raised as a comment.

Decide the language, don't dodge it

When it asks "do you mean Customer or User?", that's not pedantry — it's the skill sharpening fuzzy terms before they calcify into wrong code. Pick one. Your decision becomes canonical language in CONTEXT.md, and the next engineer inherits the precision instead of the ambiguity.

The skill stress-tests with invented edge-case scenarios on purpose. Engage the specific case, not the general shape — edge cases are exactly where wrong assumptions hide.

Steer it — a grill is a conversation, not an interview

The "interviewee" framing has a failure mode of its own: going passive — answering only what you're asked and letting the model set the entire agenda. A passive grill sprawls. It spins up more and more questions, and the scope quietly explodes past what you set out to plan. You're still the senior in the room: redirect when a branch doesn't matter ("skip that — out of scope"), volunteer a constraint before it asks, and call the session when it's done. Active steering is the difference between a grill that converges and one that wanders.

Know when to stop

The target is shared understanding, not exhaustion. When new questions go trivial, no branches remain, and you could write the implementation yourself from the answers so far — stop and say "good, proceed." Grilling past that point is its own kind of waste.

Your win for today

Run a session with the triage card open

  1. Open the Playbook beside your next real grilling session.
  2. For each question, silently name its bucket — ①, ②, or ③ — before you answer.
  3. Catch one question you instinctively started to answer that you should have told the model to verify in the code. That instinct is the habit we're breaking.
  4. Catch one bucket-③ question and answer it with an explicit reason or constraint, not just a value.
