Grilling Session Playbook

A one-page operating procedure for being interviewed well by grill-with-docs. Print it; keep it beside the terminal.

Before you start

ChoiceDo
Which modelGrill with your strongest model — grilling runs on parametric (training-baked) knowledge. Save cheaper models for implementation, which runs on contextual knowledge.
How much scopeOne change per grill. Too broad and the model drifts out of its sharp zone and hides high-fidelity questions. Ask it to chunk a big plan, then grill each chunk separately.

Triage every question into one bucket

① Verifiable → send it to the code

The answer is already in the repo (current shape, existing behaviour, how a function works). Don't recite it. Say: "Check src/… and tell me what's there." Reciting wastes the loop and risks you misremembering.

② Proposed-and-right → approve, move on

Its recommended answer matches what you'd have said. Confirm in one word and keep velocity. Most questions should land here as the plan sharpens.

③ Intent / constraint → only you know — be precise

Product intent, business rules, external API contracts, team conventions not yet written down, "we can't do X because compliance." The model cannot read these. This is the bucket your session exists for. Spend your effort here.

④ High-fidelity → prototype, then grill again

Can't be answered in words — "one screen or three?", a layout, a flow. Don't force a verbal guess. Park it, build the smallest throwaway prototype, bring the answer back. Loop: grill → prototype → grill again.

Rules of engagement

SituationDo
You reject the recommended answerSay why. "No, because…" The reason is the signal — a bare "no" makes it guess again.
It flags a fuzzy term ("Customer or User?")Decide, don't dodge. Your decision becomes canonical language in CONTEXT.md.
It invents an edge-case scenarioEngage concretely. Edge cases are where wrong assumptions hide — answer the specific case, not the general shape.
It contradicts your codeResolve it out loud. One of the two is wrong; decide which before any code is written.
You catch yourself only answering what's askedSteer. A grill is a conversation, not an interview — redirect off-scope branches, volunteer constraints, call the end. Passive grills sprawl and the scope explodes.

Stop signals — end the session

The goal is shared understanding, not exhaustion. Stop and tell it to proceed when:

  • New questions have gone trivial — you're approving every recommendation unchanged.
  • No unexplored branches remain on the design tree.
  • You could write the implementation yourself from the answers so far.

Then: "Good — write the plan / proceed." Lingering past this point is its own waste.

After you stop

  • Don't just /clear. The context is full of decisions you paid for. Implement in the same session, or make sure each decision became a glossary line, an ADR, or the written plan first.
  • Optional throughput: you can run two grills in parallel — answer one while the other is thinking. Still fully hand-driven (you steer each), just more than most people will want to track at once.
